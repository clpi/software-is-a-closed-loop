## Thesis, in plain English

A semi-persistent equality saturation engine can be made accountable at three levels at once. First, its associative and commutative congruence closure is sound and, at convergence and under stated side conditions, complete. Second, its matching engine — the reference matcher, every plan the scheduler can produce, the flattened and regrouped views, the demand rule in its corrected and per-kind and law-aware forms, the sequence steps, and the mutable match environment — is sound and complete against a reference semantics, again under stated side conditions. Third, at a saturation round that changed nothing, every match of every rule is fired, and that result lifts through the bridge to the closure so that firing covers every theory-equal node of a class. Rows that tie the model back to the running engine — the index filter, subsumption, normal forms for right-hand sides, soundness of what the actions build, and the engine stop test — are closed separately in the fidelity area. Where a plausible stronger statement is false, the development proves the failure instead of leaving it as folklore.

## Definitions carried

- Theory equality: two monomials are theory-equal when the asserted equations and the declared laws for the operator derive their equality. Class membership, congruence, and completeness are all stated relative to that relation and to a supplied law set and a supplied set of asserted equations.
- The slot invariant, called the fifth invariant in the development: each class carries a minimal monomial, and that minimum is the one the ordering on monomials selects. Preservation of that invariant depends on refreshing the minimum when a node shrinks in place, and on ordering monomials by a per-class order key rather than by representative identity.
- Completion state and round fixpoint: a completion state is converged when no rule remains to add and no critical pair remains open, together with the invariant and agreement conditions listed as premises below.
- Matching semantics: the reference matcher uses maximum partition semantics for associative and commutative patterns. A match is a binding that satisfies every atom of a pattern on a graph. Soundness means every emitted binding is a match; completeness means every match is emitted, for the plan or view or rule in question.
- The demand rule, in three forms: the original form, the corrected form in which a leaf is satisfied when it holds a taken class or its opened class is taken, and the engine form in which the opened-class clause is available for multisets only, together with a law-aware form in which a leaf also counts when closing it is not inert.
- The saturation round: a round matches on a frozen snapshot, applies its actions, and produces the next snapshot. A round is unchanged when it has the same nodes and the same class finding before and after. Fired means every insertion consequence is a present node and every union consequence is a merged pair.
- The represented graph: the matching graph of one associative and commutative operator, read from a converged completion state. The bridge theorems are stated about that reading.
- Conformance model: the conformance development contains no theorems. It is executable definitions only — generators, oracles, cases and emitters — whose output is the committed fixture programs that the engine is then run against. Its value is as a control, not as a proof.

## Examples and controls

- Conformance generators: four executable generators in the conformance development produce fixture programs for the base matcher, for flattened matching, for congruence closure, and for closure certificates, plus a rounds generator. At this pin the committed fixture families each contain 200 programs for the seeds the integration workflow regenerates and compares. The expected matches in a fixture come from the Lean reference model, so a fixture is a differential control between the model and the Rust engine, not a proof about the engine binary.
- Certificate generator, in plain English: the closure certificate generator emits a negative check only on pairs that a separating model separates, where a separating model is a valuation into a commutative monoid that satisfies the asserted pairs and the law instances and is cancellative when the operator is. A proved theorem in the closure area states that such a model separates only pairs that are not theory-equal, so a failing negative check from that generator is an engine unsoundness, by the repo's own argument.
- Reproduction programs: 20 small programs in the proofs area. Each non-control program fails an equality check on the engine before a named commit and passes after it; the control programs pass before and after. They cover the minimum slot gap, the demand gap, the five consequences of the inverse law, and two probes and an associativity incompleteness pair that are explicitly not failures. The associativity pair carries its own explanation: no terminating completion can be complete for associativity alone, because the word problem for finitely presented semigroups is undecidable. That is a stated boundary, not a defect claim against the engine.
- Saturation probes named in the status file, for the collapse of a two-item match when its two child classes merge, for regrouping past the model budget, and for the atomic and minimum-monomial spelling changes in plain mode, serve as concrete controls for premises that the theorems carry.

## Premises carried rather than proven, consolidated

1. Cancellation, in its general form, is a hypothesis of the main completeness result. The lattice development replaces it with lattice closure for the engine form, and a proved disproof, listed above, shows the general form does not follow from lattice closure. It remains carried, not discharged in general.
2. The round facts that rebuild establishes are premises of the saturation and final results: round well-formedness, merge tracking on, persistence of nodes through a round in the stated canonicalised form, nothing subsumed in the base form, and well-formedness of rules and plans. The fidelity area closes some of these rows against the engine; the status file names two that still assume recanonicalisation is a literal map, which a unit drop or a degenerate collapse on rebuild breaks.
3. Emission is a premise: the first round applies every binding of the naive run of every rule, and a later round applies every required binding for both routing branches. The theorems prove completeness from that emission behaviour; they do not prove the Rust engine code emits in that way.
4. The bridge and final results carry the convergence hypotheses of the completion state, including the law facts, the critical pair facts, congruence on nodes, and the presence of the asserted equations between active nodes of one class.
5. View and work bounds in the engine, and multiplicity widths, are hypotheses only where they appear. The regrouping completeness results do not cover the engine's own regrouping bounds or its skipping of records; the status file argues that the skipping is acceptance-preserving and states plainly that it is argued, not proved.
6. The plan results hold for any enumeration meeting the stated enumerator specification. The walk alone is proved to meet it; richer enumerations inherit the result only through that specification premise.
7. On the Rust side, the trusted body markers and axiom functions enumerated in the trust boundary chapter, and the admitted specifications inside the pinned standard library for the verifier, are premises of every Rust verification result. Ordinary Rust callers of the verified containers are not protected by the erased preconditions; the crate mitigates that with runtime checks, which are themselves outside the proof.

## Non-claims carried

- Not modelled, from the status file's own list: the view, work and element bounds and multiplicity widths as anything more than hypotheses; the galloping cursor in the Lean development, which is instead verified on the Rust side; faults inside sequence sub-queries; duplicate-freedom of emitted lists, where only membership is proved; and the build-time spelling of a right-hand side's associative and commutative term, so the saturation result is about matches, not about the nodes a right-hand side builds.
- Not modelled in the closure area: recanonicalisation by rebuild, where termination rests on a fixed alphabet; termination of the associative-only round interleaved with associative and commutative rounds and with congruence; and the one-element collapse in the insertion operation.
- Not modelled in the rounds area: flattened and sequence rules across rounds, action application, and the transport of bindings through later merges. Not modelled in the saturation area: subsumption runs in the base form, literals, globals, primitives, comprehensions, a flattened right-hand side flag, and sequence rules; n-ary right-hand sides are stored literally.
- Not covered in the regrouping area: operators with laws in the completeness results, and soundness under laws for regrouped views.
- The round-level converse in the fidelity soundness area is stated as open in the status file.
- The Lean development is a model of the engine, tied to the engine by the fidelity rows, the conformance fixtures and the reproduction programs. It is not a proof about the compiled Rust binary, and the repo does not claim that it is.
- On the Rust side, the repo does not claim that the anti-unification derived value equals the optimum, that the production solver is refined to the verified positional model, that the proof logging records in the engine are independently replay-checked certificates — the top-level overview states they are not yet — or that a negative lazy completion check is a proved semantic disequality; the overview states it reports only that the bounded search did not derive equality.
