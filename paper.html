01 Settled intent

Rulings. Each is a target construction — stating one does not claim the graph is saturated or benchmarked.

S-01  Quality space is an exact construction over equivalencesSettled intent
2026-11-17 16:10 PST · Chris ruling · Spec §16
  • Seed: a named release snapshot — its expressions, observed behaviors, contracts, premises — each with an immutable typed identity.
  • Saturate equivalence deterministically; merge only equalities valid under the same stated premises; derivation + scope preserved per edge; cross-lineage transfer only when its conditions are checked.
  • Attach quality facts per alternative — capability, effects, correctness obligations, latency, memory, build cost, other named axes — each carrying workload, environment, uncertainty, evidence.
  • Unknown is not zero and not a loss. Dominance only on no-worse on every required axis + better on ≥1, same scope. Nondominated set = the frontier. Incomparables kept. Content-ID tie-break orders display only.
  • Commit rule-set, term universe, evidence snapshot, premises, graph, frontier under typed roots in the snapshot. Retractions recompute the projection; earlier snapshot retained.
  • Completeness receipt: “entire saturated space under these rules and this universe” is sayable only on an exhaustive fixed point over a finite, explicitly defined universe with all rules fairly applied. It never implies every equivalent program is contained. A budget stop is an exact partial record, remainder unknown.
S-02  Effect accounting is derived from the witness setSettled intent
2026-11-17 16:33 PST · Chris ruling, rejecting 0-of-205 · Spec §17
  • Full accounting across the whole equality-saturated lattice, over append-only witnesses, behavior joined across equivalent provable states + environments; trivially derivable + filterable across the unified set.
  • Witnessed behavior is never voided for lacking pre-attached workload / environment / uncertainty / evidence. Parts derive from witness provenance (venue, pin, run) carried by the store itself.
  • Per-venue behavioral difference is independently sufficient signal. Only facts with no witness at all stay unknown; any underivable part is named per fact.
S-03  Accounting composes + compounds provablySettled intent
2026-11-17 16:36 PST · Compounding clause · Spec §17.8

Across every run, even a trivially measured one. Each run joins the unified set and strengthens the accounting. Every composition step carries its derivation — compounding is checkable, never asserted on trust.

S-04  Gates are three-valuedSettled intent
Living spec §1.23.9 · Spec §§1, 8 · Boundary is theorem-grade

PROVED / DISPROVED / UNRESOLVED — unresolved is a first-class verdict under shepherd policy, never a failure to decide and never silently converted. It falls to contested / uncaptured, never to absent or proved. No work item closes by removing the boundary. Every UNRESOLVED names exactly one boundary: budget (cap fired) → frontier (residue left) → rules (rule set cannot derive it), in that priority.

S-05  Framing: a joined hypergraph over a finite, intended spaceSettled intent
2026-11-17 17:15 PST · Chris framing correction · Strengthens S-01 / S-02
  • The problem space is finite: the set of behavior intended to be qualified, under-specified only with respect to the intended qualification.
  • There is no infinite space outside it — no unwitnessed, unintended, unqualified behavior beyond the boundary to appeal to.
  • The construction is a joined hypergraph across qualified software: that hypergraph is the problem space.
  • The solution — the qualification set of behavior — is a finite subspace of that hypergraph.
  • Coverage gaps are inside that boundary, never an outside. See S-06 and §8.

2026-11-17 17:20 PST · Design answer for S-08 · Proposed until ruled settled

  • Boundary sensing: executions sensed at the kernel boundary every program must cross — process lifecycle + the syscall stream, the finest universally available grain. Occupied states append and join into the semi-lattice.

S-09  Robust across foreign baggage — any hook, Idol-owned representationSettled intent
2026-11-17 17:22–17:26 PST · Chris, direct — correction: robust across foreign baggage, not free of it
“It has to be robust across foreign baggage. Idol can represent the execution environment that is most robust, most high resolution — and it should. It must provably be.”
  • Collection may use any foreign hook necessary — userland tracer, per-process / per-thread profiler, OS or kernel mechanism, whatever a given software and kernel require. No hook is refused for being foreign.
  • What Idol owns is the representation of that execution environment, and it must provably be the most robust and highest-resolution one: finite, determinable, at the highest possible physical resolution, across the highest-resolution temporal and ordering bounds the platform affords.
  • Collected once, at the boundary. The state is then known by derivation — it never has to be observed independently a second time. There is no separate observation step standing beside the collection.
  • Scope is unqualified: any software, in any language, on any kernel (S-08). The foreign trace and profiling themselves are what is collected — lifted into Idol-symbolic form, where repeated structure is shared in the graph (S-07b).
  • Proof obligation, named: “most robust, highest resolution” is a claim to prove against the platform's actual bounds — finest grain, temporal bound, and ordering bound stated per environment — not an adjective. Until that proof exists for an environment, its resolution claim stays at the state its evidence supports.
S-10  Essentially stateless — no backfill jobsSettled intent
2026-11-17 17:24–17:26 PST · Chris, direct · Supersedes the backfill mechanism in S-06 / B-01
“It shouldn't even require a backfill job. Is there not a tractable, essentially stateless way forward here?”

Answer, as ruled: yes — remove the job, not the coverage. Nothing needs to be scheduled, swept, or coordinated, because there is no backlog state to manage:

  • The witness is definitional to the execution. An execution carries its witness as part of its result, emitted by the machinery itself — for foreign software, emitted at the single boundary collection of S-09 and lifted immediately. A witness is not a record someone later goes and fetches.
  • The accounting is a pure fold over content-addressed witnesses. Its join is associative, commutative, and idempotent, so the accounting is recomputable from scratch, exactly, in any order, with no coordination state. Duplicate witnesses change nothing; arrival order changes nothing.
  • Historical records need no special path: they enter by the same derivation applied to older inputs. Same fold, same result — not a one-off import job.
  • An execution with no record is an unoccupied state in the graph, colored unresolved. It is not a queue entry. It occupies on its next execution, by the definitional rule above.
  • Evidence this is tractable, not just tidy: the independent checker in §3 (V-A) already recomputes the whole fold from scratch and matches every step hash — full recomputation is demonstrated behavior, which is exactly what a stateless fold requires.

06 Practice today — gates in production

V-F  Pair verdicts, head-compile gate, drift sentinelVerified
2026-11-17 · Fleet records, same day
  • Pair verdicts gate landings: a change is clean only when its failing set is a subset of the base's — implied qualification in practice: you inherit the base's qualifications, you may not add failures.
  • Head-compile gate: each new main head verdicted — e.g. 21d4c405 COMPILES.
  • Drift sentinel: verdict DRIFT at 91 findings (90 dirty trees, 1 clone-count note) → reconciliation retired 54 as contained, queued 36 unique-unlanded.
  • Overlap, observed: the same change is routinely judged by several gates at once (tests, verifier, compile, drift) — qualifications overlap and imply each other; the frontier rule is what keeps overlapping verdicts from being collapsed into a single score.

09 Implications — completely spelled out

This section is new in this revision. It does not add new rulings. It spells out, in plain language, what §§0–8 already entail if you take them seriously — for the person writing code, the person reviewing it, the person paying for compute, and the person asked to trust a claim. Where an implication is a direct logical consequence it is marked Settled intent; where it is a practical consequence that still needs a ruling to enforce, it is marked Proposed. Read it as: “if the proposal above is true, then the following must also be true.”

I-00  What changes, in one pageSettled intent
Implication of S-01 – S-10 · Read this first if you read nothing else in §9

The proposal replaces “we think it’s better” with “here is the frontier, here are the witnesses, here is what is still unknown — recompute it yourself.” Everything else in §9 is that sentence applied to a different job.

QuestionBefore — ordinary practiceAfter — under this proposal
Is A better than B?A score, a benchmark average, a reviewer’s impression.Only if A is no-worse on every required axis and better on at least one, on the same scope, from witnessed facts. Otherwise: incomparable or unknown — both are honest answers.
What counts as evidence?A screenshot, a log line, a number in a PR description.A witness: a content-addressed record with venue, pin, and run in its provenance, that joins the append-only ledger and survives an independent re-check.
What does “unknown” mean?Often treated as zero, as failure, or quietly dropped.A first-class state. Unknown blocks dominance, is named per fact and per axis, and is never converted to a loss or a pass.
Who checks the checker?The same tool that produced the result.An independent checker that imports nothing from the derivation. If it cannot recompute the fold and match every hash, the claim is not “checked.”
What happens to history?Old results are overwritten, imported by one-off jobs, or lost.History enters by the same pure fold over the same witnesses. No backfill jobs, no special import path, no coordination state (S-10).
What about software you didn’t write?Out of scope, trusted on its word, or excluded.In scope by construction: lifted into the graph, sensed at the kernel boundary, represented in Idol’s form at the highest resolution the platform affords (S-08, S-09).

Net effect: fewer claims, stronger claims. Most pairs today will not be decided — §4 shows 908 of 908 blocked, each for a named reason. That is not the system failing. That is the system refusing to guess, and telling you exactly which witness would unblock it.

For the authorYou cannot hand-wave a win

If you say “faster”, you must say on which axis, on which corpus, in which environment, with what uncertainty — and point to the witness. A raw time with no corpus is unknown, not a bound (A-01).

For the reviewerYou review the frontier, not a vibe

Your question changes from “do I believe this?” to “which axis blocks this pair, and is that block real?” Every block in §4 is named — effects, capability, correctness — so review becomes checkable work, not taste.

For the teamNo single score to game

There is no leaderboard number to optimise at the expense of everything else. Improving one axis while silently regressing another does not produce dominance — it produces incomparability, printed as such.

For the reader laterClaims stay recomputable

Because the fold is associative, commutative and idempotent, a reader in six months can recompute the accounting from scratch, in any order, and get the same result — or find exactly where it diverges.

I-01  For anyone who writes or changes codeSettled intent
Implication of S-06, S-07, S-10
  • Every execution you cause is a witness. Not just the benchmark you meant to run — the test run, the gate run, the trivial local run. Under S-10 the witness is definitional to the execution: it is part of the result, not a report you file afterwards if you remember.
  • “It worked on my machine” becomes data, not an anecdote. A per-venue difference is independently sufficient signal (S-02, V-E). If your machine disagrees with CI, that disagreement is a split entry with an environment signature — it is kept, not averaged away.
  • You inherit qualifications. In practice today (V-F) a landing is clean only if its failing set is a subset of the base’s. You may not add failures and call it a wash because other things improved.
  • Unfinished work stays visibly unfinished. If your change has no effects witness, no memory witness, no build-cost witness, those axes read unknown for your alternative — and they will block any dominance claim you try to make for it. The gap is printed next to your change, not buried.
  • What you do not have to do: run a backfill job, file a separate coverage report, or coordinate with anyone about ledger order. Duplicate witnesses change nothing; arrival order changes nothing.
I-02  For testing, CI, and landing changesSettled intent
Implication of S-04 · Gates are three-valued
  • CI stops being pass/fail. Every gate returns PROVED / DISPROVED / UNRESOLVED. UNRESOLVED is not a flaky failure to retry until green — it names one boundary, in priority order: budget → frontier → rules. You fix the named boundary, or you ship knowing it is unresolved, under shepherd policy. You do not silently convert it.
  • Overlapping gates are expected, not redundant. The dictation in P-00 starts here: qualifications may be implied and may overlap. Tests, verifier, compile, and drift (V-F) judging the same change at once is the normal case. The frontier rule is what stops overlapping verdicts being collapsed into one misleading score.
  • A gate that cannot name its premises cannot merge its result. Equalities merge only under the same stated premises (S-01). A green run under different premises is a different fact, not a stronger version of yours.
  • Flakiness gets a precise meaning. Two witnesses for the same fact in different environments that disagree are contested or split — carried, never dropped (C-02, §17.4). “Re-run until it passes” destroys exactly the signal the accounting is built to keep.
I-03  For performance work and benchmark claimsSettled intent
Implication of A-01 bound forms · V-E per-venue signal
  • Only proven bounds count. Latency wins as a lower upper bound; throughput wins as a higher lower bound. A single raw timing, a ratio without a corpus, a best-of-N with no spread — all enter as unknown.
  • Every speed claim must name its corpus. The headline join in V-E (cbor decode: throughput lower bound 1.44, latency upper bound 27.63 ms) is only sayable because the contract names a 41,723-byte corpus. Without that, the same numbers would be unusable.
  • Environment is part of the measurement, not metadata. V-E shows the certification environment reading systematically slower on all six shared facts. Under this proposal that pattern is derived from the differences alone — you do not need to have pre-labelled the environments for the signature to appear.
  • Uncertainty is not optional decoration. 251 idol-sites entries currently have underivable uncertainty because their witnesses record no spread (G-07). Those facts can be listed, but they cannot support a tight bound. Recording a range is what makes a performance fact usable.
  • Consequence for optimisation work: the cheapest way to “win” a comparison is often not to optimise code — it is to witness the axis that is currently blocking the pair. §8 is, read this way, a prioritised optimisation backlog: effects for idol-lib (597 pairs), capability for idol-sites (276 pairs), build-cost everywhere (no witness at all).
I-04  For choosing between two implementationsSettled intent
Implication of S-01 frontier rule · V-D worked example
  • There is no total ranking. Alternatives form a frontier, not a leaderboard. Two good implementations can both stay on the frontier, incomparable, because each is better on a different axis. Keeping both is a correct outcome, not indecision.
  • “Incomparable” is an actionable answer. V-D is the template: pristine vs. fork is incomparable — fork better on capability, pristine better on verification (1,259 vs. 1,251 verified, 0 vs. 15 summary errors). The page does not pick a winner. It names exactly what would move it: fork verification to 0 errors, and the fork dominates on the measured scope.
  • Tie-breaks do not decide. Content-ID ordering orders display only (S-01). Alphabetical, newest-first, or author preference must never be presented as a quality result.
  • Cross-project comparisons are never tested. No cross-project pairs (§16.10, C-03). “Our library is faster than their unrelated tool” is not a frontier claim this system will ever produce.
  • Decision-making consequence: if you must choose today with a blocked pair, you are choosing under named ignorance. The record shows which axis you are guessing about. That is a very different meeting from one where a single score hid the guess.
I-05  For releases — and for saying “done”, “better”, or “complete”Proposed
Implication of S-01 completeness receipt · §17.12 claim discipline
  • “The entire saturated space” is a gated phrase. It may be said only on an exhaustive fixed point over a finite, explicitly defined universe with all rules fairly applied — demonstrated by an actual final empty round, not asserted. Every receipt in §4 currently reads partial, and the phrase appears nowhere on this page. That restraint is the system working.
  • A budget stop is not a completion. If saturation stopped at node-cap, round-cap, or time-cap, the receipt is an exact partial record and the remainder is unknown. Shipping notes that omit that are misclaiming, even if the code is fine.
  • Retraction is normal and does not erase history. Retract a fact and the frontier recomputes; the earlier snapshot is retained (S-01). A release can therefore be corrected without pretending the earlier claim never existed.
  • A release snapshot is the unit of qualification. Rule-set, term universe, evidence snapshot, premises, graph and frontier are committed together under typed roots. “Which version was better?” is only answerable snapshot-to-snapshot, never floating-head to floating-head.
I-06  For foreign, legacy, and third-party softwareSettled intent
Implication of S-08, S-09 · Mechanism in S-08b is still Proposed — the scope is not
  • “Not our code” stops being an exemption. The construction must cover any software, in any language, on any kernel. A dependency, a legacy binary, a vendor tool — each is lifted into the graph and sensed at the boundary every program must cross: process lifecycle and the syscall stream.
  • You may use any hook necessary — and you own the representation. S-09 corrected an earlier, purist reading: collection may use any foreign hook a platform requires. What must be Idol-owned, and provably most robust and highest-resolution, is the representation: finite, determinable, at the highest physical, temporal and ordering resolution the platform affords, collected once and thereafter known by derivation.
  • Supply-chain consequence: a third-party component’s qualification is not its vendor’s datasheet. It is the set of witnessed behaviours of that component, in your environments, joined into the same lattice as your own code — filterable by venue, state, and project like everything else.
  • Honest limit, printed: “most robust, highest resolution” is a proof obligation per environment, not an adjective (S-09). Until that proof exists for a given kernel, that environment’s resolution claim stays at whatever state its evidence supports.
I-07  For venues, time, and compute costProposed
Implication of P-00 — Chris’s dictation, still Proposed until ruled settled
  • Venue choice is itself a Pareto problem. P-00 applies the same dominance rule to venues as S-01 applies to programs, on two objectives: time ↓ and compute ↓. A venue dominates only if it is no-worse on both and better on one. The diagram in §0 is the whole argument: A, B, C, D are all on the frontier; the hollow venues are dominated and should not be chosen for that workload.
  • “Fastest” and “cheapest” are different claims, and both can be true of different venues. Collapsing them into cost-per-run or a single cloud bill hides the trade-off the frontier exists to show.
  • Parallel solving is part of the dictation, not an optimisation. “I solve the Pareto optimal in parallel given a finite set of venues” — the solve is parallel because the venue set and the behaviour set are both finite. Finiteness (S-05) is what makes exhaustive, parallel frontier computation tractable at all.
  • Build-cost is currently the blind spot. It is a required axis, and no witness anywhere carries a recorded cost time (G-03). Until gate and bench runs carry wall time + venue in their witness, every venue comparison that needs build-cost is blocked — exactly as the frontier in §4 is blocked today on effects and capability.
I-08  For operations, incidents, and debuggingProposed
Practical consequence of S-09 (collected once) and S-10 (pure fold)
  • You do not re-observe what you already collected. State collected once at the boundary is known by derivation thereafter (S-09). An incident investigation that starts by re-running the failing thing under a different environment is destroying its own evidence — the first execution’s witness is the record.
  • Debugging becomes filtering, not archaeology. The five filter faces (§17.7) — by class, axis, venue/environment, state, project — were re-executed and matched exactly in V-A. “Show me every latency fact for this state, on this venue, that is contested” is a query, not a week of log-diving.
  • Reproducibility is a property of the fold, not of discipline. Because the join is associative, commutative and idempotent, two operators folding the same witnesses in different orders get the same accounting. “Works on my fold” is checkable in the same way a hash is.
  • Symbolic profiling is part of the same accounting — when it exists. S-06 requires an entire accounting of it. Today the spec and census are complete (SYM-01) and the profiler is unbuilt, its cells absent or unmeasured (SYM-02). That gap is printed, not smoothed over — which is itself the operational implication: you currently cannot claim profile-level explanations for those cells.
I-09  For audit, compliance, and anyone asked to trust a claimProposed
Implication of C-01 verdict records and V-A independent checker
  • An auditor does not need to trust the team, the tool, or this page. A verdict record is self-contained, in exact field order, with its record hash (§9 of the engine spec). The independent recheck program must exist before any record may be labelled “checked” — and the engine never labels its own output checked.
  • Tampering is detectable by construction. In V-A, one altered value in a copy of the fold was refused by the checker. That control is the compliance story in miniature: change a number, break a hash, get refused.
  • Provenance replaces attestation. Instead of a person signing “we tested this”, each fact carries workload, environment, uncertainty and evidence — or derives them from witness provenance (venue, pin, run). Where a part cannot be derived, it is named per fact (S-02), so an auditor sees the hole rather than a signature covering it.
  • The four states are an audit control. Settled / Proposed / Implemented / Verified, never blurred, means a roadmap item can never be read as a delivered capability without changing its label — and the label change is itself a visible act.
I-10  For product, planning, and what to build nextProposed
Reading §8 as a prioritised backlog
  • §8 is a prioritisation, whether it was meant as one or not. Rank gaps by pairs blocked: effects for idol-lib (597) → capability for idol-sites (276) → correctness for idol-lib (35) → everything in G-03–G-08. Witnessing one axis can unblock hundreds of comparisons at once; building a new feature unblocks none.
  • “Coverage” stops being a percentage someone estimates. The space is finite and intended (S-05). A gap is an unoccupied state inside that space, with a closing condition. Planning can therefore say “these 10 states, closed by these executions” instead of “improve coverage.”
  • Every run compounds — including trivial ones. S-03: even a trivially measured run joins the unified set and strengthens the accounting, with its derivation carried. There is no “too small to record” run. Conversely, an unrecorded run contributes nothing, however impressive it felt.
  • Resourcing consequence: instrumentation work (definitional witness emission, B-01) is not overhead beside the product work. Under this proposal it is what makes every other team’s claims decidable. Deferring it defers every dominance decision that depends on it.
I-11  What becomes forbiddenSettled intent
Direct negations of S-01 – S-10 · If you do one of these, you have left the proposal
  • Treating unknown as zero, as a loss, or as a pass — in a table, a chart, or a sentence.
  • Claiming dominance from a single axis, a single benchmark, or a single venue.
  • Collapsing several axes into a weighted score and presenting the score as a quality fact.
  • Using a raw time or raw ratio as a bound.
  • Dropping a contested or split fact because it is inconvenient, or re-running until it agrees.
  • Labelling your own output “checked” without an independent checker.
  • Saying “the entire space” / “all equivalent programs” on a partial, budget-stopped, or uncommitted saturation.
  • Closing a gap by deleting it, renaming it as “out of scope”, or moving it outside a space that S-05 says has no outside.
  • Scheduling a backfill job as the mechanism for coverage — the mechanism class was retired in S-10.
  • Presenting a Proposed or Settled entry as Verified, in a slide, a release note, or a sales claim.

And what becomes required instead:

  • Name the axis, scope, corpus, environment and uncertainty behind every comparative claim — or mark it unknown.
  • Keep incomparable alternatives on the frontier, printed, until a witness decides them.
  • Carry the derivation with every composition step, so compounding is checked, never asserted.
  • State the closing condition for every gap you file, in the same act as filing it.
I-12  Costs, risks, and what this does not promiseGap
Honest limits · Filing these does not weaken the proposal — hiding them would
  • It is slower to claim anything. Most comparisons will sit blocked for a long time — 908 of 908 today. Teams used to a quick “ship it, it’s faster” will experience this as friction. The friction is the point, but it is a real cost in speed of conversation.
  • Instrumentation must be built before it pays off. Definitional witness emission (S-07b) and the symbolic profiler (SYM-02) are not yet built. Until they are, witnessing is partial and many axes stay unknown. The proposal does not conjure witnesses for free.
  • Storage and derivation are not free — they are bounded differently. Symbolic witnesses share repeated structure in the graph, so maximal resolution costs the size of the derivation, not the size of a trace (S-07b). That is a strong claim about growth, and it holds only if sharing is actually implemented as specified.
  • It does not prove correctness of the underlying software. It qualifies behaviour that has been witnessed and equivalences that have been saturated under stated premises. An unwitnessed behaviour is unoccupied, not safe. A saturated graph is complete only under its rule-set and universe (S-01).
  • It does not settle P-00 or the proposed mechanisms by spelling them out. Venue-Pareto solving, lifting + boundary sensing, and the witness-hook mechanism remain Proposed until Chris rules them settled. This section must not be cited as that ruling.
  • It does not make incomparable decisions for you. When two alternatives stay on the frontier, a human still chooses — on cost, on taste, on strategy — and now must do so openly, as a choice under named ignorance rather than as a computed result.
  • Adopting it partially is coherent, but the claims must shrink with it. Use only the frontier rule, and you may claim only frontier results. Use only the witness ledger for one project, and cross-project claims stay untested by design. Partial adoption with full-size claims is the one incoherent option.
I-13  The closing test — five questions for any claim you meetProposed
For readers of this page, and of any claim made under it

When anyone — including this page — tells you something is better, faster, safer, or done, ask:

  • 1 · On which axes? Name them. If an axis is required and missing, the claim is blocked, not made.
  • 2 · On what scope and corpus? Same snapshot, same contract, same premises — or it is a different fact.
  • 3 · From which witnesses? Point to them, with venue, pin and run. No witness, no fact — only unknown.
  • 4 · Who checked it independently? If the producer is the only checker, it is not checked yet.
  • 5 · What is still unknown, and what would close it? A claim that cannot answer this is hiding its gaps. A claim under this proposal answers it by construction — the gap list is §8, and it only shrinks when an execution occupies the state.

If those five can be answered, you do not need to trust the claimant. You can recompute the frontier yourself. That — not a new dashboard, not a new score — is the implication of the whole proposal.
